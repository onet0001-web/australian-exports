Australian Exports

How commodities, changing values and international markets shape Australia’s export economy.

Australia is one of the world’s major exporters of minerals and energy commodities. This visualisation explores which commodities generate the greatest export value, how their importance has changed and which countries form Australia’s most significant markets.

01

Australia’s export leaders

A small group of minerals and energy products contributes a large proportion of Australia’s export value.

Key finding

Iron ore was Australia’s most valuable export commodity in 2024–25, reaching approximately A$116.3 billion. Its export value was substantially higher than LNG, gold and coal.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Resources and Energy Quarterly.

02

Two decades of change

Export values have expanded substantially, but commodities have not grown at the same rate or maintained the same rankings.

Key finding

Australia’s resources and energy exports increased considerably over the past two decades. Iron ore became the largest contributor, while LNG and coal contributed to the more recent export-value peak.

Key finding

Iron ore moved from second place in 2004–05 to become Australia’s leading export commodity. LNG climbed from fifth to second place, while both coal categories moved down the ranking by 2024–25.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Resources and Energy Quarterly.

Key finding

Gold and alumina recorded the strongest growth between 2023–24 and 2024–25. Iron ore remained the largest export despite declining by approximately 15.6%, while metallurgical coal experienced the largest decline among the ten selected commodities.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Resources and Energy Quarterly.

Key finding

The combined value of the top ten exports fell by about A$27.7 billion in 2024–25. Gold, alumina, aluminium and copper added A$19.7 billion, but this was outweighed by falls in iron ore, metallurgical coal, thermal coal, LNG and crude oil, which together lost A$47.4 billion.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Resources and Energy Quarterly.

Key finding

Iron ore experienced the largest long-term increase among the five commodities. LNG also expanded substantially, while coal values reached exceptionally high levels before declining. Gold rose strongly in 2024–25.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Resources and Energy Quarterly.

03

Australia’s major export markets

Asian markets dominate the trade network, with China, Japan and South Korea receiving the greatest combined export value.

Key finding

China was the largest destination for Australia’s five selected export commodities in 2023, followed by Japan and South Korea.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, based on ABS and International Trade Centre data.

Market size

China was the largest market in 2023. Japan was the second-largest market, followed by South Korea.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources.

All exports, not just resources

Across all goods and services, China took 29.4% of Australia's exports in 2025, well below its roughly 50% share of the five major resource exports. Exports to the United States rose by 46%, making it the third-largest market.

Source: Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade, Australia's top goods and services trade, 2025.

Market concentration

China received about half (49.9%) of the combined value of Australia's five major commodity exports across these markets in 2023. Together, China, Japan and South Korea accounted for more than four in every five dollars.

04

Different commodities, different markets

Australia’s major commodities do not share the same international customer base. Some are highly concentrated, while others are more geographically distributed.

Key finding

China dominates iron ore exports, while Japan is the leading market for LNG and thermal coal. India is particularly important for metallurgical coal, while gold has a more geographically varied market structure.

Blank heatmap cells mean that the country was not reported among the five leading markets for that commodity; they do not necessarily represent zero exports.

Source: Department of Industry, Science and Resources, based on ABS and International Trade Centre data.

Key finding

Iron ore exports are strongly concentrated in China, while LNG and coal are distributed across several major Asian markets. Gold has a more geographically varied group of leading destinations.

Conclusion

Australia’s export strength is substantial, but concentrated.

Australia’s export economy is strongly supported by minerals and energy products. Iron ore remains the clear leader, while LNG and gold have become increasingly important. However, the data also reveal a high dependence on a limited number of Asian markets—particularly China, Japan and South Korea.

This concentration creates significant export opportunities but also exposes Australia to changes in commodity prices, demand and regional economic conditions. Continued market diversification may help create a more resilient export economy.
